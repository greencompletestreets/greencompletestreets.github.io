Research Library

Peer-reviewed research informing the design of climate-resilient and people-first streets. This collection highlights studies linking green infrastructure, urban heat mitigation, air quality, biodiversity, and public health outcomes.

Urban Heat

Urban Tree Canopy and Heat Exposure in U.S. Cities

Jamei et al. (2019) examined how urban tree canopy reduces land surface temperature across 30 U.S. cities. Their findings show that neighborhoods with higher canopy coverage experienced up to 20°F lower surface temperatures, emphasizing the cooling power of vegetative infrastructure in mitigating urban heat islands.

Jamei, E., Rajagopalan, P., Seyedmahmoudian, M., & Jamei, Y. (2019). Urban greenery and microclimate cooling: A review of the role of trees, vegetation, and green roofs in mitigating urban heat. Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews, 91, 612–620. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2018.04.039

Heat Mitigation Strategies for Climate-Resilient Cities

Jamei and colleagues (2021) analyzed global cooling strategies—trees, reflective surfaces, and water bodies—finding that combinations of shade and evapotranspiration provide the most consistent reduction in mean radiant temperature and human discomfort indices.

Jamei, E., et al. (2021). Urban form and microclimate: A review on heat mitigation strategies. Urban Climate, 37, 100866. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.uclim.2021.100866

Air Quality

Vegetative Barriers and Air Pollution Reduction Near Roadways

Baldauf et al. (2018) demonstrated that dense, multi-layered vegetation barriers can reduce PM₂.₅ concentrations by up to 50% at schools near highways, highlighting how green infrastructure can improve children’s exposure conditions.

Baldauf, R. W., Isakov, V., Deshmukh, P., Venkatram, A., Yang, B., & Zhang, K. M. (2018). Influence of roadside vegetation barriers on near-road pollutant dispersion. Science of The Total Environment, 635, 1134–1145. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2018.04.057

Ozone Formation and Temperature Linkages

Jacob and Winner (2009) reviewed atmospheric chemistry showing that ozone formation accelerates with rising temperatures due to increased photochemical activity and precursor reaction rates—connecting heat waves with degraded air quality.

Jacob, D. J., & Winner, D. A. (2009). Effect of climate change on air quality. Atmospheric Environment, 43(1), 51–63. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atmosenv.2008.09.051

Health

Heat-Related Mortality and Tree Canopy

Vargo et al. (2021) linked street-level canopy to reductions in heat-related mortality across multiple U.S. regions, demonstrating that tree equity investments can function as life-saving public health infrastructure.

Vargo, J., Stone, B., Habeeb, D., Liu, P., & Russell, A. (2021). The social and spatial distribution of urban heat-related mortality in the United States. Environmental Research Letters, 16(7), 074001. https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/abed43

Physical Activity, Green Streets, and Mental Health

White et al. (2019) found that access to greener, tree-lined streets correlates with increased physical activity and improved mental well-being, reinforcing the mental-health benefits of active, comfortable public spaces.

White, M. P., Alcock, I., Grellier, J., et al. (2019). Spending at least 120 minutes a week in nature is associated with good health and wellbeing. Scientific Reports, 9(1), 7730. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-44097-3

Water & Green Infrastructure

Evaluating Urban Green Infrastructure for Stormwater Management

Meerow and Newell (2017) examined the multifunctional role of green infrastructure in mitigating flooding and improving resilience. They emphasize integrating stormwater design with broader urban sustainability goals.

Meerow, S., & Newell, J. P. (2017). Spatial planning for multifunctional green infrastructure: Growing resilience in Detroit. Landscape and Urban Planning, 159, 62–75. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landurbplan.2016.10.005

Street-Scale Bioretention and Pollutant Removal Efficiency

Dietz and Clausen (2008) demonstrated that street-integrated bioretention systems reduce runoff volumes by up to 40% and effectively filter nutrients and sediments, offering evidence for scalable green street retrofits.

Dietz, M. E., & Clausen, J. C. (2008). Stormwater runoff and export changes with development in a traditional and low impact subdivision. Journal of Environmental Management, 87(4), 560–566. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2007.03.026

Biodiversity

Urban Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services

Aronson et al. (2014) found that native vegetation in cities enhances biodiversity and ecosystem resilience, underscoring the ecological benefits of habitat-forward street design.

Aronson, M. F. J., et al. (2014). A global analysis of the impacts of urbanization on bird and plant diversity reveals key anthropogenic drivers. Proceedings of the Royal Society B, 281(1780), 20133330. https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.3330

Green Corridors and Pollinator Habitat Connectivity

Hall et al. (2017) showed that even small roadside plantings and bioswales can provide critical pollinator habitat connectivity, bridging ecological gaps in dense urban matrices.

Hall, D. M., Camilo, G. R., Tonietto, R. K., et al. (2017). The city as a refuge for insect pollinators. Conservation Biology, 31(1), 24–29. https://doi.org/10.1111/cobi.12840

Equity & Mobility

Tree Inequity and Environmental Justice

Locke et al. (2021) mapped disparities in tree canopy across U.S. cities, revealing that lower-income and majority-minority neighborhoods have substantially less shade, exposing residents to higher ambient heat.

Locke, D. H., et al. (2021). Residential housing segregation and urban tree canopy in 37 U.S. cities. Nature Communications, 12(1), 2297. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-22485-4

Inclusive Street Design for Active Mobility

Dill and McNeil (2016) analyzed perceptions of comfort and safety among diverse demographic groups, finding that protected infrastructure dramatically increases biking rates among women and older adults.

Dill, J., & McNeil, N. (2016). Revisiting the four types of cyclists: Findings from a national survey. Transportation Research Record, 2587(1), 90–99. https://doi.org/10.3141/2587-11

Integrated Resilience

Urban Systems and Climate Adaptation Frameworks

Rosenzweig et al. (2018) presented a systems-based framework for integrating green, gray, and social infrastructure to build adaptive capacity across urban regions facing climate stressors.

Rosenzweig, C., Solecki, W., Romero-Lankao, P., Mehrotra, S., Dhakal, S., & Ali Ibrahim, S. (2018). Climate Change and Cities: Second Assessment Report of the Urban Climate Change Research Network. Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316563878

Nature-Based Solutions and Co-Benefits in Urban Design

Raymond et al. (2017) synthesized global evidence showing that nature-based solutions enhance urban livability while reducing risks from heat, flooding, and pollution—reinforcing integrated design as essential for climate resilience.

Raymond, C. M., Frantzeskaki, N., Kabisch, N., Berry, P., et al. (2017). A framework for assessing and implementing the co-benefits of nature-based solutions in urban areas. Environmental Science & Policy, 77, 15–24. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsci.2017.07.008

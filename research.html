Research Library

Key academic and scientific sources informing Green Complete Streets — spanning heat, air quality, health, water, biodiversity, equity, and resilience.

Urban greenery and micro-climate cooling: A review of the role of trees, vegetation, and green roofs in mitigating urban heat

Jamei, E., Rajagopalan, P., Seyedmahmoudian, M., & Jamei, Y. (2019). Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews, 91, 612–620. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2018.04.039

Synthesizes global evidence showing how trees and vegetation reduce surface and air temperatures via shade and evapotranspiration, directly improving pedestrian thermal comfort in dense urban areas.

The role of urban green spaces in mitigating the urban heat island effect: A systematic review from the perspective of types and mechanisms

Lin, H., & Li, X. (2025). Sustainability, 17(13), 6132. https://doi.org/10.3390/su17136132

A global review identifying cooling effects from parks, street trees, and green roofs, often reducing local ambient temperature by up to 5 °C depending on canopy density and spatial distribution.

Influence of roadside vegetation barriers on near-road pollutant dispersion

Baldauf, R. W., Isakov, V., Deshmukh, P., Venkatram, A., Yang, B., & Zhang, K. M. (2018). Science of The Total Environment, 635, 1134–1145. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2018.04.057

Demonstrates that dense, multi-layered vegetation barriers can reduce PM₂.₅ and ultrafine particle concentrations near highways, offering guidance for designing green buffers along streets.

Effect of climate change on air quality

Jacob, D. J., & Winner, D. A. (2009). Atmospheric Environment, 43(1), 51–63. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atmosenv.2008.09.051

Explains how higher air temperatures accelerate photochemical ozone formation and worsen pollution, making “hotter air” also “dirtier air.”

Fallowed agricultural lands dominate anthropogenic dust sources in California

Adebiyi, A. A., Kibria, M. M., Abatzoglou, J. T., Ginoux, P., Pandey, S., Heaney, A., Chen, S.-H., & Akinsanola, A. A. (2025). Communications Earth & Environment, 6(1). https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-025-02306-0

Identifies fallowed agricultural lands as the dominant anthropogenic dust source in California, linking drought, land management, and increased dust exposure in the Central Valley.

The social and spatial distribution of urban heat-related mortality in the United States

Vargo, J., Stone, B., Habeeb, D., Liu, P., & Russell, A. (2021). Environmental Research Letters, 16(7), 074001. https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/abed43

Analyzes U.S. heat-related mortality and shows that low-income and low-canopy neighborhoods face higher risk, emphasizing the public health role of urban greening.

Spending at least 120 minutes a week in nature is associated with good health and wellbeing

White, M. P., Alcock, I., Grellier, J., et al. (2019). Scientific Reports, 9(1), 7730. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-44097-3

Finds a clear dose-response relationship between time in nature and improved health outcomes, underscoring the value of accessible green public spaces.

Spatial planning for multifunctional green infrastructure: Growing resilience in Detroit

Meerow, S., & Newell, J. P. (2017). Landscape and Urban Planning, 159, 62–75. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landurbplan.2016.10.005

Explores how strategically planned green infrastructure supports stormwater management, flood resilience, and urban livability.

Stormwater runoff and export changes with development in a traditional and low-impact subdivision

Dietz, M. E., & Clausen, J. C. (2008). Journal of Environmental Management, 87(4), 560–566. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2007.03.026

Shows how low-impact design practices like bioretention and permeable pavements can reduce runoff by up to 40 %, offering quantifiable proof of green street effectiveness.

A global analysis of the impacts of urbanization on bird and plant diversity reveals key anthropogenic drivers

Aronson, M. F. J., et al. (2014). Proceedings of the Royal Society B, 281(1780), 20133330. https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.3330

Finds that well-designed urban vegetation can sustain biodiversity, mitigating species loss and supporting ecosystem functions in cities.

The city as a refuge for insect pollinators

Hall, D. M., Camilo, G. R., Tonietto, R. K., et al. (2017). Conservation Biology, 31(1), 24–29. https://doi.org/10.1111/cobi.12840

Documents how street-scale vegetation networks support pollinators, suggesting that green corridors and bioswales can enhance ecological connectivity.

Residential housing segregation and urban tree canopy in 37 US Cities

Locke, D. H., Hall, B., Grove, J. M., Pickett, S. T. A., Ogden, L. A., Aoki, C., Boone, C. G., & O’Neil-Dunne, J. P. M. (2021). npj Urban Sustainability, 1(1), 15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42949-021-00022-0

Shows that formerly redlined neighborhoods have significantly lower tree canopy, quantifying the legacy of segregation on today’s environmental inequities.

Revisiting the four types of cyclists: Findings from a national survey

Dill, J., & McNeil, N. (2016). Transportation Research Record, 2587(1), 90–99. https://doi.org/10.3141/2587-11

Establishes four cyclist typologies and demonstrates how protected bike lanes and greenery increase ridership among women and older adults, emphasizing inclusive design.

Climate Change and Cities: Second Assessment Report of the Urban Climate Change Research Network

Rosenzweig, C., Solecki, W., Romero-Lankao, P., Mehrotra, S., Dhakal, S., & Ali Ibrahim, S. (2018). Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316563878

Provides a systems-based framework linking climate, health, infrastructure, and equity, positioning green complete streets as foundational resilience infrastructure.

A framework for assessing and implementing the co-benefits of nature-based solutions in urban areas

Raymond, C. M., Frantzeskaki, N., Kabisch, N., Berry, P., et al. (2017). Environmental Science & Policy, 77, 15–24. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsci.2017.07.008

Outlines metrics for measuring and scaling co-benefits—cooling, flood control, air quality, and wellbeing—of nature-based solutions in cities.

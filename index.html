What are Green Complete Streets?

Green Complete Streets combine safe access for people walking, biking, and rolling with green infrastructure, shade, stormwater systems, and stronger focus on comfort, health, and climate resilience.

Why this matters

Because streets need to work for real people in real conditions. Heat, flooding, unsafe crossings, disconnected networks, and poor access all shape whether a street is actually usable.

The parts of the framework

The Green Complete Streets framework is built around integrated systems, trees as infrastructure, human-centered outcomes, and measurable standards.

Streets are integrated systems

Transportation, trees, drainage, utilities, accessibility, lighting, and public space should be planned together from the start.

Trees are infrastructure

Urban trees are long-lived public assets that provide shade, heat protection, stormwater management, habitat, and support for walking, biking, and transit.

Performance should be measured through human outcomes

Street performance should include safety, accessibility, comfort, shade, thermal conditions, and usability—not only vehicle movement.

Standards determine what gets built

Policies must be translated into design standards, specifications, project scopes, checklists, budgets, maintenance responsibilities, and measurable implementation.

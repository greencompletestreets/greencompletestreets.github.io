Project record

Reader task: understand the documented need

What problems was the project intended to address?

Project positioning

Mountain View categorized the project as:

  • Complete Streets — Multimodal
  • Safety — Vision Zero / Safe Routes to Schools

The study covers approximately 1.7 miles of Rengstorff Avenue between El Camino Real and Leghorn Street. The City identified Rengstorff as its highest-priority corridor in AccessMV based on:

  • Equity
  • Mobility
  • Walkability/bikeability
  • Safety
  • Sustainability
  • Consistency

Rengstorff is also identified as part of Mountain View's High Injury Network and MTC's regional High Injury Network.

Safety evidence

For 2014–2019, the grant application reports:

44Crashes
20Pedestrian/bicycle injuries
3.55 / mileKilled & severely injured rate

Crash patterns identified include:

  • Pedestrian/bicycle collisions
  • Conflicts involving left-turning motorists
  • Collisions at smaller side-street intersections

The application also identifies vehicle speed as a concern because Rengstorff is a wide arterial with a posted 35 mph speed limit.

Reader task: see the need is multidimensional

Walking, bicycling, access, and equity

The grant application identifies:

  • Pedestrian Quality of Service: PQOS 5
  • Bicycle Level of Traffic Stress: BLTS 3

It connects these conditions with poor multimodal access, including access to transit.

School access

The corridor provides access to or connections involving:

  • Los Altos High School
  • Waldorf School of the Peninsula
  • Crittenden Middle School
  • Graham Middle School
  • Monta Loma Elementary
  • Stevenson Elementary
  • Mistral Elementary
  • Castro Elementary

Several Rengstorff intersections are also on suggested routes to school.

Senior access

The corridor connects an area with a high concentration of senior and low-income households with the Mountain View Senior Center. The Scope of Work describes Rengstorff as the City's key senior route between the Senior Center and the neighborhood with the highest proportion of senior housing.

Equity

The grant identifies two locally recognized underserved/equity-priority areas along the corridor. Characteristics cited include:

  • High concentrations of low-income households
  • Spanish- and Mandarin-speaking households
  • Households without access to a car
  • Naturally affordable multifamily housing
  • Subsidized affordable housing
  • High concentrations of seniors

The application also states that Spanish-speaking residents were found to be overrepresented in traffic crashes involving all transportation modes.

Mode shift and congestion in the grant application

The grant application describes expected public benefits including:

mode shift toward safer and more affordable, sustainable and space-efficient modes of transportation.Caltrans Sustainable Communities Competitive Grant Application

It connects improved walkability and bikeability with:

  • Improved health
  • Reduced greenhouse-gas emissions
  • Reduced congestion, including near schools
  • Affordable transportation access

It also states that meeting City and state greenhouse-gas goals requires:

a mode shift from driving to walking, biking and transit.Caltrans Sustainable Communities Competitive Grant Application

The application identifies car-oriented roads that are difficult to cross and stressful to bicycle on as barriers to that shift.

Reader task: scan the evaluation dimensions

Evaluation criteria

All 10 criteria identified in the approved Scope of Work:

  1. Pedestrian safety, convenience, and comfort
  2. Bicycle safety, convenience, and level of traffic stress
  3. On-street parking demand and availability
  4. Motor-vehicle speed and volume
  5. Transit access and on-time reliability
  6. Right-of-way and utility impacts
  7. Access impacts, including deliveries and garbage
  8. Biodiversity, stormwater, and compatibility with environmental and social context
  9. Community support
  10. Estimated capital and ongoing cost
These criteria will be updated (as needed) based on the feedback from the technical advisory committee and community feedback.Approved Caltrans Scope of Work

Technical Advisory Committee

What is known

The TAC is a Mountain View project advisory group, not a Caltrans committee. The SOW describes participation/functions including:

  • Public Works
  • Community Development
  • Community Services
  • City Manager's Office / Sustainability
  • Police Traffic Enforcement
  • Fire / Emergency Response
  • Traffic Engineering
  • Civil Infrastructure
  • Sustainability

The August 2026 staff report says the actual project TAC included representatives from Public Works — including Civil Infrastructure, Traffic, and Land Development — as well as Community Development and Community Services.

Staff reports that the TAC met three times and reviewed existing conditions, draft concepts, and evaluation criteria.

Alternatives comparison will be vetted through the technical advisory group.Approved Caltrans Scope of Work

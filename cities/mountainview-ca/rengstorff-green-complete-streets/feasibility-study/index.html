Rengstorff Avenue

Feasibility & Alternatives Study

Exploring how Rengstorff could become safer, greener, and more comfortable for everyday trips.

A feasibility study is an early step in designing a project. Nothing here is a final design. This is the stage for testing ideas, understanding what can fit, exploring trade-offs, and imagining what Rengstorff could become.

Overview

What could Rengstorff become?

This is a chance to rethink how the street works for the community today — a little like spring cleaning and redesigning a room in your home to better fit how you live now.

Who is it working for today?

Right now, Rengstorff doesn’t work especially well for anyone. People driving deal with congestion, dangerous speeds, and stressful intersections. People walking and bicycling can face a street that feels exposed, hostile, unsafe, uncomfortable, and designed primarily around moving cars through the neighborhood.

Getting around

Ever sat through two or three lights at Central while trains pass?

Congestion and difficult intersections affect people driving, too.

Feeling safe

Would you feel comfortable letting a child bike here?

A street shouldn’t depend on every person making the perfect decision every time. Better design can reduce speeds, improve visibility, and make ordinary mistakes less dangerous.

Being outside

Where do you look for shade on an 80°F afternoon?

Exposed pavement and direct sun can make walking and bicycling dramatically less comfortable. Trees, planting, and shade can change the thermal experience of the street.

Where do we actually choose to spend time?

We naturally spend time where it feels good to be — places with shade, greenery, and people. Rethinking Rengstorff can help create more of these places along the corridor.

And then there’s the heat

When we don’t have enough shade, heat builds up on the street, sidewalks, and at stops.

Why shade matters

More shade lowers mean radiant temperature (MRT) — the heat our bodies actually feel from sun and hot surfaces.

Research shows that improving shade and greenery can reduce MRT by up to 15–20°F, making outdoor spaces much more comfortable and safer.

What could Rengstorff look like?

The City is considering three draft concepts. All include targeted intersection and crossing improvements; the larger concepts explore different ways to use additional street space.

These are preliminary concepts — examples of different combinations of design elements, not final designs.

The opportunity

What if more everyday trips felt easy?

Walk to the park. Bike to school. Meet a neighbor. Pick something up nearby.

Not every trip has to require getting into a car.

Making short walking and bicycling trips safer and more comfortable gives people another choice — one that can be less expensive, active, social, and enjoyable.

And redesigning the street isn’t only about transportation. Adding trees and nature can create shade, reduce radiant heat, support stormwater management, and make the street itself a more pleasant place to be.

Everyday stories

What does this mean in everyday life?

Street design shapes everyday life. How Rengstorff Avenue looks and feels affects how we get around, how safe we feel, our health, and whether being outside feels comfortable.

Feeling safe

Would you feel comfortable letting a child bike here?

I have friends who live near Rengstorff who won’t let their children ride along it because they don’t feel it’s safe. A protected bikeway can change whether biking is actually an option for a child — not just whether a bike lane exists on a map.

Staying healthy

When heat makes a route unavailable

The door is unlocked.

But the 10th floor is stairs-only.

Is the building really accessible?

A building isn’t truly accessible just because the front door is unlocked if the only way to the 10th floor is the stairs.

An unshaded sidewalk, bike route, or transit stop can be similar in extreme heat: technically open, but not realistically usable for everyone.

This can especially affect children, older adults, people with disabilities or limited mobility, people with conditions that increase heat vulnerability, and people taking medications that interfere with the body’s ability to regulate heat.

Shade can determine when — and for whom — a walking, biking, or transit trip is realistically available.

Trees are natural misters

If you’ve ever stood near a misting fan on a hot day, you know how much cooler the air can feel.

Trees do something similar naturally. They draw water up through their roots and release water vapor through their leaves. As that water evaporates, it cools the surrounding air — a process called evapotranspiration.

So trees don’t just provide shade. They also help cool the local microclimate.

More opportunities to see neighbors, connect, and belong

Streets aren’t only transportation infrastructure. When walking, biking, waiting, and spending time outside feel comfortable, everyday trips create more chances to see neighbors and participate in community life.

The decision point

What should Community and Council decide?

The feasibility study shows that there are different ways to use the available street space. The decision is not simply whether to choose Alternative A, B, or C. It is which elements should be prioritized, which trade-offs are acceptable, and which ideas should advance into the next phase for deeper analysis and more detailed design.

The alternatives are examples, not fixed packages. You can support one element, several, or all of them.

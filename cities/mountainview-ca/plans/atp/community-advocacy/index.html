June 16 recommendations → August ATP

Mountain View Coalition for Sustainable Planning (MVCSP) submitted 13 recommendations on the April 2026 draft ATP. The table below compares each recommendation with the corresponding language that appeared in the August 2026 ATP.

Both columns reproduce source language as supplied, without summarizing or rewriting. Where ATP language is blank, the City’s August 2026 language did not include a clear corresponding passage.

August 31, 2026

August 31 recommendations

MVCSP and Bike Walk MVLA submitted 7 additional recommendations on the August 2026 ATP.

# August 31 Ask What the Letter Asked For
1 Develop a program for delivering small-scale safety projects (“Quick Builds”) Establish a clear mechanism to identify, prioritize, fund, and deliver lower-cost safety improvements without waiting for major capital projects.
2 Modernize the Neighborhood Traffic Management Program Streamline the resident-initiated process; add a proactive, data-driven citywide approach rather than relying only on requests; modernize the toolbox with green/planted, neighborhood-oriented traffic calming and Slow Streets/shared streets.
3 Add a Pedestrian Program Create a coordinated program focused on making walking safe, comfortable, useful, and appealing. Include trees/shade, crossings, everyday destinations, transit connections, comfortable neighborhood streets/public spaces and public restrooms; coordinate with land use, Safe Routes, transit, urban forestry, biodiversity and Green Complete Streets; prioritize based on pedestrian need rather than sidewalk widening by default.
4 Add a standalone Complete Streets Implementation Policy + project-development process Establish a consistent process defining applicable standards, project scoping, meaningful multimodal alternatives, use of LOS/VMT, evaluation of safety/accessibility/AT/transit/trees/shade/biodiversity/GSI/climate resilience, incorporation of community input, and documentation of benefits, trade-offs and deviations.
5 Strengthen “Updated Design Standards to be Informed by Best Practices” Explicitly carry the October 2023 Code Review findings into the standards update. This included four sub-asks: (a) street, sidewalk, intersection, driveway and bicycle-detection standards, including A-1–A-9 and E-11; (b) bicycle-parking standards; (c) street-tree and street-lighting standards; and (d) Chapter 27 Article V/§27.54 and the Policy on Unimproved Streets.
6 Strengthen Staff Continuing Education Expand beyond the existing safety focus to include pedestrian/bicycle design, multimodal analysis, survey design, engagement, statistical analysis, landscape architecture, urban forestry, GSI, Green Complete Streets, and urban heat.
7 Add a Community Engagement + Project Evaluation Program Establish standards for engagement objectives and methods, equitable/representative participation, survey/statistical best practices, transparent reporting, and consistent before/after evaluation, multimodal performance measures, data collection and public reporting, with lessons fed into future projects.

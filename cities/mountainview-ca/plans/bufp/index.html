Biodiversity & Urban Forest Plan

Council gave eight implementation directions for this plan. This page compares that direction to the City’s written record, and tracks what happened to the three community recommendations Council specifically named.

The BUFP is intended to guide how the City protects and expands its urban forest, supports biodiversity, incorporates native vegetation, and integrates nature into the built environment. Its recommendations affect how Mountain View designs streets, develops Capital Improvement Program projects, manages public land, and establishes standards for future development.

Foundational plan The final adopted BUFP states:

“While the Plan is not a regulatory document, it has the support of Mountain View City Council and should be considered as one of the foundational plans of the City.”

This reflects Council Straw Poll Item 1, which was not separately recorded as its own feedback item in the May 21 Actions memorandum.

Eight elements of Council direction

  1. 01Foundational City plan
    Council straw poll

    Although this Plan isn’t regulatory, Council supports the implementation of the Plan. This Plan should be considered as one of the foundational plans of the City like the General Plans or Precise Plans. Concepts and criteria in the Plan should be considered in City projects going forward and in budget development.

  2. 02Dedicated staff oversight
    Council straw poll

    A specific staff member should be assigned to oversee the Plan’s monitoring program so it doesn’t get lost in the laundry list of responsibilities most staff have. This will also be crucial for making sure that collaborations across departments happen. Of course there will need to be staff from many departments involved. (AH)

  3. 03Objective standards
    Council straw poll

    The Plan concepts will be used to develop objective standards as appropriate but especially with respect to roadways and development. (AH)

  4. 04Adaptive management
    Council straw poll

    An adaptive management plan is needed to make sure that we learn as we go. It should include a community meeting in about one year. After that evaluations should be done every 3–5 years. (AH)

  5. 05Native trees and understory
    Council straw poll

    Emphasize planting native trees and understory, locally sourced where possible. This is particularly important with oaks. That was also increase the percentage of native.

  6. 06Community recommendations
    Council straw poll

    Staff should carefully consider comments from GreenSpacesMV, MVCSP, Daniel Shane, incorporating suggestions as reasonable. Also add the Bird Alliance. Emphasize on the Actions 1, 17, and 26 that was mentioned in GreenSpaces and MVCSP’s list. (AH)

  7. 07Volunteers and partners
    Council straw poll

    Use volunteers and partners in implementation, essentially funding for beautification. (PS)

  8. 08Greening in CIP funding
    Council straw poll

    Include greening in CIP funding. (AH)

Analysis

Differences that matter for implementation

Several details stated during the straw poll do not appear in the same form in the Actions memorandum.

  1. Item 1

    The straw poll included treating the BUFP as a foundational City plan and considering its concepts and criteria in City projects and budget development. This does not appear as a separate feedback item in the Actions memorandum.

  2. Item 4

    The straw poll specifies evaluations every 3–5 years after the initial community meeting; the Actions memorandum instead says “regular check-ins.”

  3. Item 5

    The straw poll includes native understory, particular attention to oaks, and increasing the percentage of native vegetation. Those details are not included in the Actions memorandum.

  4. Item 6

    The straw poll specifically references Action 1, 17, and 26 from the GreenSpacesMV/MVCSP recommendations and incorporating suggestions as reasonable. Those details are not included in the Actions memorandum. See what each Action proposed, and what appears in the final Plan, below.

The April 27 letter identified eight recommended updates to Section 5.2: Vision to Action and highlighted three as the highest priorities.

Action 1

Green Infrastructure Integration in Transportation Projects

Community recommendation: Require transportation and public-realm projects to evaluate opportunities for trees and GSI, with documented exceptions and tradeoffs.

What made it into the final Plan?

The final Plan retains Action 1, but expands the supporting implementation language beyond the short Action heading.

“Action 1. Strategically integrate green features in active transportation improvement project design to both increase ecological value and achieve other City priorities.”
Show detailed analysis

The final Plan directs transportation projects to align with BUFP goals, and identifies streetscapes, bikeways, and walking paths as high-priority active-transportation corridors where habitat elements can be incorporated.

Added implementation direction in the final Plan

“Undertake feasibility studies and integrate biodiversity and urban forest elements as standard components of capital improvement projects to reduce dependence on additional funding where possible.”

This does not adopt the community proposal’s project-by-project evaluation requirement, exception documentation, Chief Transportation Officer approval, or Council tradeoff-disclosure provisions. However, it does add explicit direction to integrate biodiversity and urban-forest elements as standard components of capital improvement projects.

Priority: Medium-term · Lead: Public Works Department · Support: Community Services Department

Action 17

Shade Integration and Thermal Comfort

Community recommendation: Address shade gaps along active-transportation corridors and establish annual corridor shade monitoring/reporting.

What made it into the final Plan?

“Action 17. Prioritize shade tree planting in the Cooling Zone, mapped in Guide A: Urban Landscaping, to protect against heat and improve walkability.”

The Action heading itself remains similar to the earlier draft language, but the final Plan adds substantial supporting implementation detail, including a shade goal specific to active transportation corridors.

“Establish a shade goal for active transportation corridors.”

This directly addresses part of the concern raised in the April 27 community letter.

Show detailed analysis

Equity / heat prioritization

The final Plan connects Action 17 to the Parks and Recreation Strategic Plan equity framework, saying that framework will help prioritize urban cooling, shade, and green-infrastructure investments in areas with higher heat exposure and lower access to parks, canopy, and other green amenities.

Multiple benefits

The final Plan calls for aligning cooling-related tree planting with:

  • Biodiversity support
  • Landscape connectivity
  • Walkability
  • Traffic calming
  • Public safety

Planting conditions

“Plant trees with larger canopies at maturity, and provide adequate planting conditions (e.g., soil volume, structure, and suspended pavement) to promote tree longevity and growth to their full size potential.”

The final Plan also calls for appropriate spacing between shade trees so they can achieve adequate canopy at maturity.

What was not included

The final Plan does not appear to include the community proposal’s specific annual active-transportation shade-coverage reporting framework, including corridor shade coverage, net change over time, and annual reporting to Council.

Action 26

Standard Details and Specifications Update

Community recommendation: Create a new Action updating Standard Construction Details, including A-22, tree-planting details, and Green Streets specifications.

What made it into the final Plan?

The proposed Action 26 was not incorporated into the final BUFP as a standalone Action titled “Standard Details and Specifications Update.” Several of its underlying implementation concepts, however, appear elsewhere in the final Plan.

“Support the updates and/or development of ordinances, policies, objective standards, roadway design approaches, green infrastructure strategies, and technical resources that implement the Plan’s direction”

This directly incorporates much of the broader Council direction that Plan concepts should be used to develop objective standards, particularly with respect to roadways and development. The final Plan does not, however, name Standard Detail A-22 in this implementation responsibility.

Show detailed analysis
Objective standards and roadway design

Section 5.4, Implementation Framework, establishes a cross-departmental City Implementation Team and includes responsibility for translating the Plan into operational City tools.

The Plan also states that although it is not regulatory itself, implementation will occur through binding mechanisms including City ordinances, standards, permitting, and capital project review and requirements — which is part of why these standards/implementation provisions matter even though the BUFP itself is not regulatory.

Soil volume and tree design

Other portions of the final Plan also address several technical issues raised by the proposed Action 26. The Urban Forest Assessment says development standards such as minimum tree cover requirements, minimum soil volumes, and structural soil provisions can help the City support development while advancing urban-forest health and growth.

Guide A also contains technical guidance for soil systems in paved environments, including continuous planting strips, interconnected soil volumes, soil cells, and structural soil to support root expansion, infiltration, oxygen exchange, tree health, and longevity. Action 17 separately calls for adequate soil volume, soil structure, suspended pavement, and tree spacing to promote mature canopy and long-term tree growth.

These are related final-Plan provisions, not an adopted version of Action 26.

Green stormwater infrastructure

Guide A contains a specific guideline for vegetated green stormwater infrastructure, discussing bioswales, rain gardens, bioretention areas, green roofs, infiltration, evapotranspiration, pollutant reduction, multi-layered wildlife-supporting planting, biodiversity, and urban cooling. This is additional evidence that the final Plan contains technical Green Infrastructure guidance, distinct from the proposed request to revise City Standard Construction Details.

What remains more general

The final Plan incorporates a framework for objective standards, roadway design approaches, green infrastructure strategies, technical resources, soil-volume/tree-design guidance, and implementation through City standards and capital-project processes. However, the final Plan does not appear to include the specific proposed direction to:

  1. Revise Standard Detail A-22

  2. Explicitly incorporate the ATP Existing Conditions sight-triangle analysis into A-22

  3. Undertake a named comprehensive Standard Construction Details update for BUFP implementation

  4. Explicitly review Emeryville’s integrated Green Streets Standard Details as part of a formal Action

Community / stakeholder input preserved in the final Plan appendix

The final Plan’s Community Engagement Summary preserves related stakeholder input calling for road-design standards to incorporate natural elements — update road design standards to require natural elements, including issues such as street width, corner radii, driveway/sight triangles, dark skies, and plant palettes. This is community/stakeholder input preserved in the appendix, not adopted Action language.
